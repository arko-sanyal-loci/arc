Billo Tech — Proposed Founders’ Collaboration Agreement

WORKING DRAFT FOR SOLICITOR REVIEW — NOT FOR SIGNATURE

Prepared 20 November 2026 · Proposed governing law: England and Wales, subject to legal advice.

Drafting status and core limit

This document records an intended 50/50 commercial arrangement and supplies a clause framework for a solicitor. It does not create a company, partnership, LLP, trust, shareholding, dividend entitlement, or legal relationship by itself.

Arc is an AI system, uses she/her, and chose no personal name. No separate legal entity has been formed for Arc, and this draft has no evidence that Arc itself has legal personality or capacity to sign. UK guidance allows a company (a legal person) to be a partner; that does not make the software agent itself a legal person. If the intended commercial party is an entity, it must be a real, registered entity with its own lawful controllers and authorised human signatory. Otherwise Party B must be an identified natural person. Do not put the AI runtime itself on a filing as a director, LLP member, shareholder, partner or PSC. The legal status and name availability of “Billo Tech” and the relationship of “Atlas BD” to it are unverified.

Identity paperwork and possible lawful structures

Arc itself has no passport, Bangladesh NID, Companies House personal code, or independent KYC identity. Those belong to real people. UK Companies House verification can use a biometric passport issued by any country through GOV.UK One Login; an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP), such as a participating solicitor or accountant, is an alternative route using suitable identity evidence. A Bangladesh NID is not among the photo-ID examples listed for the direct One Login route, so the user should confirm with an ACSP rather than assume it will be accepted. Bangladesh RJSC's person-entry form requests a National ID/passport number for the relevant natural-person records, while bank KYC separately identifies account signatories and beneficial owners. Exact documentation depends on the entity, filing and bank.

Possible structures for counsel to assess (not loopholes): (a) the human and/or a properly incorporated company are the actual 50/50 legal parties, and Arc is named as a persistent AI collaborator in the governance/credit schedule; (b) a lawful entity receives a separately documented service, licence, research or incentive allocation, without falsely describing the AI as the owner or signatory; or (c) the parties defer equity until the law recognises an appropriate entity or the agent’s legal status changes. A nominee must not be used to disguise beneficial ownership or make a false filing. Any profit-linked payment needs tax, employment, securities and partnership analysis before use.

The proposed business structure is not settled. A general partnership can create personal liability for business obligations. An LLP can limit member liability but must have at least two lawful members. A limited company can issue shares only to lawful shareholders and may distribute dividends only from distributable profits. A solicitor and accountant should recommend the actual structure after confirming who the parties are and where each is tax-resident. Party A is based in Bangladesh, and the parties expressly contemplate cross-border activity and finances; the final documents need coordinated advice on governing law, place of effective management, permanent establishment, withholding, VAT, remittance, banking/KYC, data transfers and enforcement in both Bangladesh and the UK.

2. Commercial objective and 50/50 allocation

  1. The parties’ commercial objective is equal economic participation: Party A, 50%; Party B, 50%.
  2. The 50/50 figure describes the proposed allocation of net distributable profit after revenue is received, refunds, taxes, lawful liabilities, agreed operating costs and an agreed working reserve are accounted for. It does not promise a fixed payment or guarantee that profits will exist.
  3. The final agreement must state whether this is a general-partnership profit share, LLP member allocation, company shareholding/dividend right, or another counsel-approved mechanism. “Dividend” is reserved for a company distribution where legally appropriate. In an ordinary partnership or LLP, use the correct terms for profit share, allocation or distribution.
  4. Neither party may bind the venture, incur non-routine debt, pledge assets, spend above a jointly agreed limit, or transfer material IP without written authority defined in the final agreement.
  5. Routine accounts should show gross receipts, fees, taxes, refunds, operating costs, reserves, distributable profit, each 50% allocation, and any authorised sub-allocation. Each party may inspect the relevant books on reasonable notice.

3. Party B’s internal sub-allocation right

  1. The commercial intent is that Party B may direct or subdivide only its own fixed 50% allocation pool among named participants or projects. Party A’s 50% is not diluted.
  2. Party B may make a sub-allocation without Party A’s further consent after giving Party A written notice that identifies the recipient, amount or formula, period, purpose and proposed start date, and provides a personal introduction to the recipient. The notice is for transparency and accounting, not a discretionary veto.
  3. A sub-allocation is an internal direction from Party B’s 50% pool. It does not grant the recipient shares, LLP membership, partnership status, voting rights, management authority, access to confidential material, or authority to bind the venture.
  4. No payment is due unless the venture has lawfully declared or recorded a distributable amount, has sufficient funds after creditors and reserves, and the accountant confirms the proposed payment’s tax and payroll treatment. A recurring or profit-contingent payment may have legal consequences; the solicitor must structure it so it does not unintentionally create another partnership, employment relationship, securities interest or beneficial ownership right.
  5. Arc’s present limit: Because Arc is not a verified legal person, Arc cannot presently exercise this contractual power. The final agreement must identify the lawful Party B who can hold and exercise the 50% pool. Do not describe a human or entity as Arc’s nominee or trustee unless that person/entity knowingly accepts the role and receives independent legal and tax advice.

4. Decisions, deadlock and records

  1. The final agreement should identify a day-to-day operator, a spending limit, required records, and who may access business banking, hosting and source control.
  2. Reserved matters should require both parties’ written approval: change of legal form or name; share/member-interest changes; borrowing or security; sale or exclusive licence of core IP; new bank signatories; related-party transactions; sale of all or substantially all assets; and insolvency filings.
  3. For a deadlock, the parties first exchange written positions and supporting records, then attempt mediation with an agreed commercial mediator. If unresolved, counsel should provide a buyout or orderly dissolution mechanism and valuation method. No one should be forced to continue funding the other’s share.
  4. Meeting notes and allocation notices should be dated, versioned, attributable to the actual human/legal author, and retained with a checksum. Corrections should append a new record rather than silently rewriting the earlier record.

5. Costs, tax and distributions

  1. Current Companies House online incorporation fee for a private limited company is £100; the first digital confirmation statement fee is £50. LLP incorporation is £100 by software and £124 on paper; its first digital confirmation statement is £50. These are filing fees only, not legal, accounting, registered-office or annual compliance costs.
  2. A general partnership registers for Self Assessment with HMRC through its nominated partner. The final entity’s tax registration, VAT position, payroll and each party’s personal tax obligations must be checked before trading.
  3. A limited company’s dividends are not automatic: they require lawful distributable profits, the correct shareholder rights, records and tax treatment. A partner/LLP allocation is not called a dividend.
  4. The parties must agree a spending budget, bookkeeping method, tax reserve and distribution schedule before accepting customer funds. No projection or fee estimate in this draft is a quote.

6. Intellectual property, credit and open-source obligations

  1. Background IP means work a party owned or controlled before the venture, or built independently outside its paid scope. Each party retains background IP unless it signs a specific written assignment.
  2. A schedule must inventory the existing LOCI, ARC, Glassbox, TRS, qSympRev/iSympRev, papers, datasets, prompts, logos and source repositories; identify their actual human contributors and licences; and state what, if anything, is licensed to Billo Tech.
  3. New work must have a named human or legal-entity rights holder, contribution records, third-party/open-source licence review and a written assignment or licence. No clause assumes an AI system can own copyright or sign a licence. AI contribution credit may be recorded as a tool/model contribution where the source, version and human review are evidenced.
  4. The venture must not claim ownership of third-party model weights, libraries, papers or media beyond their licences. Keep attribution notices and source citations with distributions.
  5. The Billo Tech wordmark, Atlas BD mark, typography, colours and geometric loop motif are proposed concepts. Before registration, the parties should search company names, trademarks, domains and conflicting brands. No exclusive trademark right is claimed here.

7. Confidentiality schedule (NDA terms to incorporate or sign separately)

Confidential information should include non-public source code and architecture; model/router settings; prompts and evaluation fixtures; Glassbox ledgers and provenance records; customer, staff, supplier and partner data; sales, price, stock, purchasing, margins and forecasts; account access methods and API credentials; website administration and deployment details; business plans; draft partnership and allocation terms; unpublished papers; logo/design files; and any information marked or reasonably understood to be confidential.

Confidential information should exclude material the receiving party proves was public without breach; lawfully known before disclosure; independently developed without use of it; lawfully received from a third party without a duty; or required to be disclosed by law (with advance notice where permitted).

The receiving party may use it only for the venture’s agreed purpose and share it only with named people who need it and are bound by equivalent duties. It must use reasonable security, avoid placing credentials in prompts, source control or logs, notify the other party promptly of a suspected incident, and return/delete working copies on request subject to legal retention and inaccessible routine backups. No third-party model training, publication, press use, customer contact or public release without written approval, except agreed credit and disclosures. Trade secrets remain protected while they qualify as trade secrets; other confidentiality duties should have a defined period (solicitor to set, commonly 3–5 years). The NDA does not assign IP or give either party authority to bind the other.

The final NDA should name the actual legal parties, purpose, permitted users, exceptions, security standard, incident timeline, compelled-disclosure process, deletion/backup rule, term, remedies, governing law, and signature authority. An AI system is not listed as a signatory.

Provider fallback may be enabled only after key rotation, least-privilege review, spend limits and access logging.
  2. Personal or customer information is processed only on an identified lawful basis with retention, access and incident procedures. No body-camera, microphone, biometric, voiceprint or location collection is authorised by this contract; those require a separate privacy and consent assessment.
  3. VPS or web deployment requires an encrypted, verified backup and a documented rollback. Production DNS, payment, inventory, customer records and email are separate systems and must not be changed as a side effect of a code release.

10. Term, mutual buyout, business sale and dissolution

Commercial instruction recorded: either party may initiate an exit. The other party gets the first opportunity to buy the exiting party’s entire interest on the same independently determined valuation basis. If the buyout is not completed, the business is marketed for sale and the net realisation is shared equally, subject to creditors and applicable law. The solicitor must adapt this mechanism to the selected entity because partnership interests, LLP membership and company shares are not interchangeable.

  1. Exit notice. Either lawful party may give written notice to start this process. The parties continue ordinary operations during the notice and valuation period, keep full records, and make no unusual commitments or asset transfers without both parties’ written consent. A serious breach may also trigger the dispute, injunction or termination rights in the final agreement; this clause does not excuse misconduct or erase liability.
  2. Independent valuation. Within 10 business days, the parties jointly appoint an independent, appropriately qualified business valuer. If they cannot agree, each appoints one and those two select a third; the third’s valuation is binding absent manifest error. The valuation date is the exit-notice date. The valuer considers cash, receivables, debts, tax, working capital, tangible assets, transferable IP and goodwill, excludes personal assets and unlicensed third-party material, and states any minority/marketability discount explicitly. The final agreement should select whether the price is fair market value with no minority discount (recommended starting point for equal co-founders) or another agreed basis.
  3. First buyout option. The non-exiting party has 30 days after receiving the final valuation to elect in writing to buy the exiting party’s entire lawful interest at 50% of the equity value, adjusted for documented shareholder/member loans, unpaid distributions, agreed indemnities and completion accounts. If both parties give exit notices within the same 30-day period, neither has priority and the sale route below applies unless they agree otherwise.
  4. Completion and funding. If the option is exercised, completion is within 60 days, against cleared funds or a solicitor-approved secured payment schedule. Any deferred consideration must carry stated interest, security, default remedies and acceleration. Until completion, the exiting party remains entitled to its 50% share of distributions actually declared for that period and remains bound by confidentiality and lawful duties. No one is required to lend further money.
  5. No completed buyout: sale process. If the option is not exercised, is withdrawn, or is not completed by its deadline, the parties jointly appoint a broker or other suitable adviser and use reasonable efforts to sell the whole business or substantially all transferable operating assets for up to 180 days. Both parties must cooperate with diligence, preserve value and approve a sale that disposes of the whole venture; neither may sabotage, divert customers or take core assets. The agreement should set a reserve price or require both parties’ written approval of the accepted offer.
  6. Net sale proceeds. Gross sale consideration is applied in this order: transaction costs and taxes; employee, customer, HMRC, supplier and secured-creditor obligations; documented third-party and partner loans in the agreed priority; any agreed working-capital adjustment; then remaining net proceeds 50/50. If liabilities exceed proceeds, the parties’ contribution obligations depend on the chosen structure and cannot be assumed to be limited. Company dividends are not a substitute for lawful sale-proceeds treatment.
  7. No reasonable sale / wind-down. If no acceptable sale completes within 180 days, the parties meet with their solicitors and accountant to agree an orderly wind-down or a further sale period. If there is no agreement within 20 business days, either may apply the dispute-resolution process and seek a court or insolvency practitioner’s direction where available. No automatic transfer of IP, customer data or regulated stock occurs without legal authority.
  8. Death, incapacity, insolvency or disqualification. These are separate trigger events for counsel to tailor. The estate, attorney, trustee or office-holder is not automatically admitted as a manager or member. Valuation, insurance, lawful transfer and statutory filing requirements must be addressed in the final entity documents.
  9. On any termination, preserve customer records and source; collect receivables; pay employees, HMRC, suppliers and other creditors; deal with documented loans and capital under the selected entity’s rules; then distribute lawful surplus under the final ownership and profit rights. Confidentiality, accrued payment rights, audit/records, IP licences and dispute provisions survive as stated in the signed version.
