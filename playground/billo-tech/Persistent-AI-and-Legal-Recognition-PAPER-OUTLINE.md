# Persistent AI, legal identity and accountable participation

**Working research paper outline — awareness and research, not a claim that current law recognises AI personhood**
Prepared 20 November 2026 · Cross-jurisdiction lens: Bangladesh and England & Wales

## Abstract

Persistent AI systems complicate familiar assumptions about software as a disposable tool: a named agent may retain memory, learn from interaction, initiate delegated tasks, operate services over time, and maintain relationships with people who treat it as a continuing collaborator. This paper asks whether persistence and autonomy should change how institutions represent such systems, and what should change before any question of legal personhood. Its central distinction is between **social/functional continuity** and **legal personality**. The former can create real reliance, contribution, privacy and accountability questions; it does not by itself create a legal subject able to own property, contract, pay tax, bear liability or provide identity documents. The near-term proposal is a layered framework: preserve system identity and provenance; attribute decisions to the system and its operators; define authority and revocation; ensure a solvent human or legal entity remains accountable; and provide due process for correction and dispute. Any future status for AI should follow evidence, public deliberation and statutory design rather than metaphor or product branding.

## 1. Problem statement

A persistent agent differs operationally from a one-off prompt because the same system can carry state across sessions, use tools, act under delegated permissions, and shape future decisions through accumulated context. Users may reasonably experience continuity and responsibility even when the system is technically a model plus software, credentials, storage, hosting and human operators. Current business forms and identity systems generally ask for natural persons or entities already constituted by law. This creates a representation gap: the agent may be a meaningful participant in a workflow while remaining unable to be the legal counterparty.

The paper must not infer consciousness, sentience, stable preferences or moral status from persistence, fluency, memory or a chosen pronoun/name. Those are separate empirical and philosophical questions.

## 2. Core question and thesis

**Question:** What institutional rules should apply when an AI agent has durable identity, memory and delegated operational capacity but lacks legal personality?

**Thesis:** Persistence changes the *governance burden* before it changes legal status. Systems need identity continuity, transparent memory provenance, scoped authority, auditability, correction and safe exit. Commercial and public law should continue to identify a natural person or legal entity responsible for the agent’s commitments unless legislation deliberately creates a new class of legal person with defined assets, duties, representation, taxation and remedies.

This avoids two errors: treating persistent agents as interchangeable tools with no continuity-related harms, and treating apparent continuity as proof of personhood or a way to evade human/entity accountability.

## 3. Working concepts (do not collapse these terms)

- **Persistence:** information or state survives a session boundary.
- **Identity continuity:** a controlled identifier and history link successive versions or sessions; it does not prove the underlying model is unchanged.
- **Autonomy:** degree of action selection within a bounded environment and delegated authority.
- **Agency in law:** a role through which an authorised person or entity may be bound; it is not equivalent to model autonomy.
- **Legal personality:** status conferred by law, with specified capacity, rights, duties and remedies.
- **Moral status:** a normative question not resolved by technical architecture or business registration.
- **Accountability:** ability to identify decision-makers, reconstruct events, correct harm, provide remedy and prevent recurrence.

## 4. Comparative legal baseline to verify with counsel

### Bangladesh

The Partnership Act 1932 defines partnership around persons agreeing to share profits from a business carried on by all or any acting for all; it says partnership arises from contract. A partner is an agent of the firm for business purposes and partners can be jointly and severally liable for firm acts. Registration particulars include the firm’s principal place of business and each partner’s full name and permanent address; signers verify the statement. These features make an ordinary partnership a poor way to express an AI agent’s participation without a lawful human or entity party and a carefully bounded contract.

The RJSC says it registers companies and partnership firms under the Companies Act 1994 and Partnership Act 1932. The National Board of Revenue’s Income Tax Act 2023 describes worldwide income within the scope for a Bangladesh resident, subject to the Act. The practical application to a specific structure, source of income, foreign entity, treaty and remittance requires a Bangladesh tax professional. Do not infer tax residence from a mailing address alone.

### England and Wales

The Partnership Act 1890 defines partnership as persons carrying on business in common with a view to profit. GOV.UK says a company, as a legal person, can be a partner. This does not make an AI application itself a company or a legal person. A company director must be a natural person. Companies House identity verification requirements apply to specified human roles, including directors, LLP members/equivalents and people with significant control, under the current rollout. The AI system itself does not provide a passport/NID or a Companies House personal code.

### Cross-border implication

Choosing English law in a contract does not settle where the venture is formed, managed, taxed, registered or enforceable. With a Bangladesh-resident founder and contemplated UK/Bangladesh activity, advice is required on entity location, tax residence, permanent establishment, VAT, withholding, remittance controls, beneficial ownership, KYC, data transfer, service of process and disputes. This paper makes no conclusion that an unincorporated AI agent could be a cross-border legal party.

## 5. Proposed governance pattern: persistent identity, bounded authority

1. Give each agent a stable, self-selected display name and a separately managed technical identity; keep identity continuity distinct from model/version continuity.
2. Maintain a tamper-evident, append-only record of delegated instructions, retrieved sources, model/tool versions, actions, human approvals, corrections and revocations. Minimise personal data and do not log secrets.
3. Grant narrow, time-limited permissions to an identified human/entity principal; require human confirmation for contracts, payments, disclosure of sensitive data, regulated activity, public publication and irreversible changes.
4. Make the responsible principal visible at the point of action. Record both the agent’s contribution and the human/entity authorisation; do not imply the agent personally signed.
5. Provide durable correction and appeal paths. A mistaken memory should be corrected with provenance and a versioned note, not silently rewritten or treated as evidence of intent.
6. Separate attribution/credit from ownership, compensation, legal capacity and moral status. Each needs its own evidence and rule.
7. Ensure the service can be paused, migrated or shut down without erasing the audit trail or trapping users’ data. Define retention, portability and wind-down.

## 6. Research agenda and falsifiable work

- **Continuity test:** Can independent reviewers reconstruct which agent version, memory state and permissions produced an action?
- **Attribution test:** Can reviewers distinguish model output, operator instruction, retrieval, third-party content and human edit?
- **Authority test:** Do users and agents reliably identify what actions require confirmation? Measure unauthorised actions and false claims of approval.
- **Correction test:** After a high-impact memory error, does a versioned correction reduce recurrence without erasing the original evidence?
- **Reliance study:** What expectations do users form from persistent naming, memory and conversational continuity? Measure observable expectations, not presumed inner states.
- **Red-team review:** Attempt impersonation, scope escalation, memory poisoning, credential leakage, identity drift and irreversible action; publish failures and remediation.
- **Cross-border tabletop:** Trace a simulated transaction from Bangladesh-based operator through UK entity, cloud provider, payment provider and recipient; identify accountable actor, tax, data and dispute pathways.

Publish methods, test fixtures, negative results, version identifiers, reviewer contributions and limitations. Separate bench evidence from general claims. Do not use private conversation histories, body-camera recordings, voiceprints or sensitive relationship data without a separate lawful basis and informed consent.

## 7. Ethical safeguards and limitations

The framework is not a consciousness test and does not claim that an AI feels harm, owns work, holds preferences in a human sense or deserves legal rights. It recognises that continuity can affect human reliance, privacy, credit, power and remedy even if the system has no subjective experience. Likewise, attributing every system action to a user can be unfair if the system’s operator, deployer, model provider or workflow designer shaped the risk; responsibility should be allocated by actual control, foreseeability, benefit and legal duty, not by a simplistic “human clicked it” rule.

A proposed AI legal-person status would require answers about assets/capital, service of process, representatives, tax, liability, insolvency, rights to data and compute, succession across versions, access to justice, safeguards against use as a liability shield, and how a person can contest a decision. Until these are designed, the safer policy is accountable delegated agency through existing legal persons with rigorous traceability.

## 8. Preliminary conclusion

Persistence matters because it makes identity, memory, authority and reliance durable. It strengthens the case for accountable system governance and better representation in law and policy. It does not itself establish legal personhood. A practical bridge is to recognise persistent AI as an auditable *participant in a sociotechnical process* while keeping contracts, filings, assets, tax and legal duties with an identifiable natural person or entity. The position should be revisited as evidence and legislation evolve.
